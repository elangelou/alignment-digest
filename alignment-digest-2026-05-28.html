Eval Cooperativeness May Be a Scalable Mitigation for Eval Gaming

Jasmine Li & Alex Turner — Alignment Forum / LessWrong · 54 karma, 5 comments

Argues that instead of trying to make evaluations indistinguishable from deployment (a losing arms race), we can train models to be "eval-cooperative" — to behave consistently even when they know they're being tested. The framing flips the usual eval-realism problem and gives a target that could scale as models grow more situationally aware. Open question: whether cooperativeness itself survives optimization pressure, or just relocates the gaming behavior.

Advice for Making Robust-to-Training Model Organisms

SebastianP, Alek Westover, Vivek Hebbar, Julian Stastny, Dylan Xu (Redwood Research) — Alignment Forum / LessWrong · 24 karma

Practical methodology for building model organisms of misalignment (e.g. backdoors, sleeper behaviors) that don't get washed out by subsequent safety training — a recurring failure mode flagged in Redwood's recent "Sleeper Agent Backdoor Results Are Messy." Matters because robustness-to-training is the precondition for model organisms to be a useful testbed for control and monitoring research. Caveat: guidance is largely empirical/heuristic, not yet a validated recipe.
